Putting it into words

Average Rate and Rate Right Now

We've now seen this idea twice: with a battery, and with a generator. Let's name it clearly before moving on.

Average Rate

Average rate tells us how fast something changed, spread evenly over the whole time period. For example: a keke travels 10 kilometres in 20 minutes. On average, it covered half a kilometre every minute, even though it was faster in some moments and slower in others.

Rate Right Now

Rate right now tells us how fast something is changing at one exact moment, not over the whole journey. It's like a speedometer: it shows your speed at this instant, not your average speed for the whole trip.

Now, in your own words: what information do we need to describe how quickly something changes? And how is "rate right now" different from "average rate"?

To describe how quickly something changes, we need three things: the quantity that changed, the size of that change, and the exact time interval over which it happened. Average rate divides the total change by the total time. It's a summary over the whole stretch. Rate right now measures the change at one specific moment, the way a speedometer shows your speed right now rather than your average speed for the whole trip.
Beyond the equations

Focusing on the Big Picture

Don't let mathematical notation intimidate you. Every one of these ideas answers one central, real-world question:

How fast is this quantity changing, right now?

Whether it's tracking the cost of producing bags of garri, or a vehicle's speed in traffic, calculus gives us the precision to answer that question exactly.

Remember. Calculus is not about memorising symbols. It's about understanding continuous change.
